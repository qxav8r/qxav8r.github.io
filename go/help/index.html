Quick start

LogTen Pro is the record. Floggy-Go doesn't keep anything: each page reads one eCrew (or Flighty) file in your browser and hands its part of the flights to LogTen. Every page builds the same match key for a flight (date + flight number + route), so they all land on the same LogTen flight and fill it in a little more each time, instead of adding copies. It's the same idea as the Floggy Mac app, just without Floggy's data file in the middle: the records come together in LogTen.

PageWhat it adds to the record
ScheduleThe month's flights, deadheads and duties, with scheduled (IBT) times, crew, duty on/off and P1/P2, plus calendar files. Use it first: it builds the records.
PairingOne trip: legs with crew and duty times, plus the trip rig and calendar files. Handy for a single trip or a change mid-month.
FlifoOn the road: actual Out, your In and the tail, leg by leg from Alaska's flight status.
FlightyActual Out/In and tails for a whole year from your Flighty export.
LogbookThe final word: actual times, tail, landings, P1/P2 and Part 121/91 from eCrew's Pilot Logbook. Send it last.
  1. Save the report from eCrew (or Flighty).
  2. Open the matching Floggy-Go page and choose the file.
  3. Check the legs. Uncheck any you don't want.
  4. Tap Send to LogTen. LogTen opens and imports.

What "beta" means: the Floggy-Go tools work and are in daily use, but they're still being tested and improved. Look over the imported flights in LogTen before you rely on them, and tell us in the Floggy Facebook group if something looks wrong.

Order: Schedule (or Pairing) first to build the records, Flifo or Flighty along the way for actual times, and the Logbook last: it has the final say on P1/P2 and Part 121/91. Sending a page again later just updates the same flights.

Best results: import on any device, but review and tidy your records in LogTen Pro for Mac on a large display. Seeing the whole month's flights side by side makes gaps and odd legs easy to spot, and it's where your full record lives.

Your files never leave your device. The pages read them in the browser and hand the flights straight to LogTen.

Getting the CSV from eCrew

Each Floggy-Go page needs its own eCrew report, saved as a CSV file:

  • Schedule: the Personal Crew Schedule Report, one bid month (or a custom date range).
  • Logbook: the Pilot Logbook report for the dates you want. A year at a time keeps it tidy. Keep each report within one base.
  • Schedule: eCrew → Schedule → My Schedule → Print, then the file/export icon → Save as Text (.csv).
  • Logbook: eCrew → Reports → Pilot Logbook Report, then Save as Text (.csv).

On iPad the file goes to the Files app (usually Downloads). On Mac it goes to your Downloads folder. Then pick it with the Choose … CSV button.

Saving eCrew files on iPad

  1. Make a folder once: in the Files app, under On My iPad or iCloud Drive, make a folder such as eCrew.
  2. Save there: when Safari asks where to save the report, pick that folder. Or set Safari to always use it: Settings › Apps › Safari › Downloads.
  3. Choose from there: Choose … CSV opens Files; tap Recents or your eCrew folder.
  4. Tidy up: once LogTen has the flights, the report has done its job. Delete old ones (touch and hold › Delete), or keep a month or two in case you want to send again. iPad adds "-1", "-2" to repeated names, so check the date before picking.

Use eCrew in Safari, not the eCrew iPad app: the app doesn't save the same CSV files.

Put Floggy-Go on your device

iPad

  1. Open the Floggy-Go page in Safari.
  2. Tap Share (the square with the up arrow) → Add to Home Screen.
  3. From then on, launch it from its icon like an app.

Mac

  1. Open the Floggy-Go page in Safari.
  2. Bookmark it, or use File → Add to Dock.
  3. The Choose … CSV button opens a normal file window.

The screen, top to bottom

  1. Header shows which version you're in (Schedule or Logbook) and its version number and date.
  2. Choose … CSV picks your report. The file name appears beside it. Until you pick one, an example file is loaded so you can see how things work. Nothing from the example is yours.
  3. Info banner shows the report period and how times are read. Logbook reminds you that logbook times are in base time: if you changed bases, pull a separate report for each base.
  4. Summary boxes show the pilot, base and seat, when the report was generated, and counts of flights, deadheads, sims, ground, reserve and warnings. Check that the name and dates are what you expect.
  5. Options ▸ (tap to open) holds the switches that control what gets sent (next section). The line beside it lists anything changed from the defaults.
  6. Leg cards: one line per leg or duty, grouped by block and day (Schedule; Logbook also by month), duty day (Pairing) or month (Flighty). Checked legs are sent. Tap a leg to see its details.
  7. Send box holds Send to LogTen and Copy LogTen URL. A big send comes in parts of 100.
  8. LogTen package (tap to open) shows exactly what Send passes to LogTen. You can ignore it unless you're curious.

Options

All four import pages (Schedule, Logbook, Pairing, Flighty) use the same options, in the same order; a page only shows the ones that fit its report. The highlighted button is the current choice. The defaults suit most pilots, so you can leave them alone. Switches change only what is sent this time. Nothing is saved.

The leg cards

Each leg is one line: checkbox, date, flight, route and times. Scheduled → actual appear together once the leg has flown (a small +1 means the next day). A tag shows anything that isn't a normal flight, and ⚠ marks a leg with a warning.

  • Tap a leg to open its details: crew, P1/P2, duty times, tail, type, remarks, the match key and any warning in full. Legs with warnings start open.
  • Schedule and Logbook: legs are grouped by day, and days by block: days in a row, treated as a trip. A block ends after a day off or a day that ends at base, so back-to-back turns are separate blocks. Logbook puts months on top. Each day shows its date, number of legs and kinds of duty.
  • Show: Blocks · Days · All sets how far the list opens: just the blocks (the default), the days inside them, or every leg. You can still tap any header to open or close it.
  • Pairing groups by duty day (Show: Days · All), Flighty by month (with Open all / Close all for the legs).
  • Checkboxes on every header check or uncheck everything inside, so it's easy to pick one block or a single day. All at the top checks or unchecks everything; the count shows how many will be sent. A header in the warning color has a leg with a warning inside.
  • Only checked legs are sent. A few start unchecked on their own: duty codes Floggy doesn't recognise, legs missing an airport, and (with Non-flying set to Leave out) ground, reserve and deadhead duties.

Sending

  • Send to LogTen opens LogTen and imports the checked legs. LogTen adds new flights and updates ones it already has.
  • More than 100 legs (a year from Flighty, say) are split into parts, each with its date range. Send each part in turn and let LogTen finish before the next.
  • If LogTen doesn't open, tap Copy LogTen URL, paste it into Safari's address bar and tap Go.
  • You can send the same report again at any time, for example after flying the trip. Flights are updated, not duplicated.
  • Flights you've locked in LogTen are skipped without any message.
  • Nothing reaches LogTen until you tap Send.

Below the send box, two folds do more with the same checked legs: Export CSV (every import page) and Add to Calendar (Schedule and Pairing).

For the technically curious

  • Schedule follows Floggy 2.6's Parse Schedule and Log10 Export rules, with the duty code list built in. Logbook follows Parse LogBook and Log10 Export.
  • The airline code, when chosen, goes on the flight number only. LogTen's flight key stays the bare number, so imports match the ones Floggy sends.

Export CSV for another logbook

For pilots who keep ForeFlight, Logbook Pro, MyFlightbook or a spreadsheet instead of (or as well as) LogTen. It's on Schedule, Logbook, Pairing and Flighty, and works the same on each.

  1. Check the legs you want, as for LogTen. The export uses only the checked legs.
  2. Tap Export CSV for another logbook to open it, and choose a logbook. Nothing is picked until you do.
  3. Check the preview table: it's exactly what goes in the file. Read the notes under the picker: what that logbook needs, and what this report can't supply.
  4. Tap Download (or Copy CSV). On iPad it goes to Files › Downloads; on Mac, your Downloads folder. Then import it in your logbook.
LogbookGood to know
ForeFlightUses ForeFlight's two-table template (Aircraft + Flights), every column kept. Dates YYYY-MM-DD, clock times hhmm, total time decimal. Import on ForeFlight on the Web (Logbook › Import). Needs a tail on every flight.
Logbook ProNo fixed columns: map each one once in the Import Wizard (File › Import Wizard) and save it as a template. Make custom fields (flight number, crew, deadhead) and the aircraft type first.
MyFlightbookColumn names match MyFlightbook's import exactly (Logbook › Import). Block, scheduled and duty times are UTC; total time decimal. Needs a tail on every flight.
SpreadsheetPlain columns for Excel, Numbers or any logbook that maps columns on import (Safelog, mccPilotLog and others).
  • Options in the fold depend on the logbook: time zone (UTC or station local), duration and date formats, extra columns, and whether to include deadheads, sims and ground/reserve rows. Flights are always in.
  • Each report has gaps. Schedule has no tails or Part 121/91 (enter one tail for every leg, or fix tails later); Logbook has the PIC name only and no duty times; Pairing is one trip per file; Flighty has no crew or P1/P2. The fold tells you which apply.
  • Imports add, they don't update. Unlike LogTen, these logbooks add every row as a new flight. Export each month or trip once, or you'll get copies.
  • Legs not yet flown have no actual times.
  • Your logbook choice and settings are remembered on this device.
  • File names follow Floggy's pattern: name, crew ID, logbook and date range, e.g. SMITH-12345_ForeFlight_2026-11-01_to_2026-11-30.csv.

Add to Calendar

Schedule and Pairing write calendar files, the same events as Floggy's Cal – ICS. (Logbook and Flighty don't: they're about flights already flown.)

CalendarSchedule (whole month)Pairing (one trip)
FlightsOne event per leg at scheduled times, like ✈ PDX FAT AS 2286, crew in the notes.
DutyReport to release for each duty day, legs in the notes.
Trip / DayA trip with a pairing number is one all-day banner across the trip, named by the number (4150). Any other day is one all-day event, named first and last city (PDX SEA), or the duty code (LB) on a ground-only day. The schedule report has no pairing numbers, so Schedule shows day events; Pairing shows its trip as a banner and the rest of the month by day.
  • On iPad: tap a button, then Add All and pick the calendar. On Mac: the file downloads; open it to add it to Calendar.
  • Schedule adds upcoming legs only (today on); earlier days and legs already flown are left out. Preview events lists what's in each file.
  • Import again any time. Each event has the same ID Floggy gives it, so a schedule change updates events instead of adding copies.
  • Times follow the time zone. Events are written in UTC, so they show in your iPad's current time zone: on a Hawaii trip, a 07:00 Pacific report shows as 05:00. To always see Pacific time, turn on Settings › Apps › Calendar › Time Zone Override.
  • Keep the three apart: put Flights, Duty and Trip / Day in separate calendars (e.g. "QX Flights", "QX Duty") so you can show or hide each, and so Flighty only sees flights (see Closing the circle).

Pick the calendar every time. iPad puts imported events in your Default Calendar unless you choose another in the Add sheet. Set it to your flying calendar once: Settings › Apps › Calendar › Default Calendar.

Floggy-Go Flifo

Floggy-Go Flifo looks up a flight on Alaska's public flight status and sends the actual out time, in time and tail number to LogTen. Use it on the road to fill in a trip as you fly it, after importing your schedule.

  1. Type the flight number. The date starts at today; change it for a flight on another day. Alaska keeps about 3 days either side of today.
  2. Tap Get Status. Each leg of that flight number appears, with its status, tail and times.
  3. Fix the ATA (In) time (see below), uncheck any leg that isn't yours, and tap Send to LogTen.

Alaska shows On, not In

Alaska's flight status gives the actual departure (Out) but its arrival time is the On (touchdown) time, not block-in. Your logbook needs block-in, so every leg that has departed gets an editable ATA (In) box. Once the flight has landed it is prefilled with Alaska's On time; before that (or if Alaska has no time yet) it is empty, so you can type your In at the gate or leave it empty to send just the Out. Change it to your actual block-in time before sending, e.g. 16:12 or 1612 (24-hour, station local). If you send it unchanged, LogTen gets the touchdown time and your block time comes out a few minutes short. Past midnight is handled for you.

IBT vs ORA scheduled times

The scheduled times in eCrew are the IBT contract times. They don't always match the ORA times Alaska publishes to the public, and that difference is on purpose: the contract times are the ones that count for rig and pay.

  • Include scheduled times (STD/STA) is off by default, so Flifo never touches the scheduled times already in LogTen from Floggy-Go Schedule or Floggy.
  • Turn it on only for a flight that has no eCrew times in LogTen, for example an Alaska or Horizon flight you rode that isn't in your eCrew schedule. It sends Alaska's public (ORA) times, not IBT times.

What gets sent

Good to know

  • Matching: LogTen updates the flight with the same date, flight number and route, the same way Floggy-Go Schedule and Floggy build them, so Flifo fills in the flights you already imported. Anything without a match is added as a new flight.
  • Overwrites: Out, In and tail replace what LogTen has for that flight. Crew, P1/P2, duty times and everything else are left alone.
  • Swapped aircraft: if the tail changed after you sent a planned tail, run Flifo again after departure; the actual tail replaces the planned one.
  • Check the date late at night or away from base: it defaults to your device's date.
  • Flifo reads Alaska's public flight status page. If Alaska changes that page, Flifo may stop working until it's updated.

Floggy-Go Pairing

Floggy-Go Pairing reads one pairing saved from eCrew and does three things with it: works out the trip rig (what the trip should pay), sends the legs to LogTen, and writes calendar files. It is the browser version of Floggy's Calculate Rig, Parse Archive and Cal – ICS.

  1. In eCrew (Safari), open the pairing's Pairing Details.
  2. Tap Share → Options → Web Archive → Save to Files.
  3. In Floggy-Go Pairing, tap Choose pairing file and pick that file. It stays on your device.

Trip rig

The big number is Trip Pay: the best of the contract rigs (MDCR, 50% Duty, 25% TAFB), with total block (CBLK) shown for reference. The REG bucket is the daily credit RainMaker should show. How each rig was worked out, How trip pay works and Definitions at the bottom of the page explain the math in plain language.

  • A yellow Check this one box means a leg moved to another day (delay or IRROPS) or a time is still an estimate. The rig reflects the trip as flown; if you saved the original trip, run it too and compare.
  • The 4:12 daily minimum doesn't apply to training, company business, functional flights or trips you split by trade or drop, so the result can run high on those.

Send to LogTen

The page opens with the trip summary (pairing number, dates and route), then folds, all closed to start: Pairing legs, Rig, Rest of the month (Dashboard), Send to LogTen, Add to Calendar and Export CSV. Each fold's header shows a short summary (Trip Pay is in the Rig header), and the page remembers which ones you leave open. Legs are grouped by duty day; tap a leg for its crew, duty times and tail, and uncheck any you don't want. Send to LogTen uses the same matching as Floggy-Go Schedule and Logbook (date + flight + route), so it updates the flights you already imported instead of adding copies. Trip legs also carry the pairing number (LogTen's Custom Note 2, the same field the Floggy app fills); single duties such as LB, reserve or sim don't. As with Schedule, send the Logbook import last: it is the record for P1/P2 and Part 121/91.

Options (tap to open) holds the switches, in the same order as every other import page (see Options). The line beside it lists any you've changed from the defaults.

Crew: behind the scenes, eCrew keeps a list of flights and crew, but only for the current schedule period (the month today falls in). A pairing saved from any other month, past or future, has no crew data no matter how you save it, and a trip that crosses into another month has crew only for the current month's legs. The leg's warning says so. For those trips, crew comes from Floggy-Go Schedule (or the Logbook for the PIC).

Floggy-Go Flighty

Floggy-Go Flighty reads the CSV that Flighty exports and sends your flights to LogTen: actual out and in times and the tail, one year at a time. It is the browser version of Floggy's Parse Flighty.

  1. In Flighty, go to Settings → Export Flights and save the CSV to Files.
  2. In Floggy-Go Flighty, tap Choose Flighty CSV and pick it.
  3. Tap a year (newest first). Uncheck any flight you don't want and tap Send to LogTen. A big year is sent in parts of 100; send each part in turn.
  • Include scheduled times (STD/STA) is off by default, as in Flifo: Flighty has the airline's public (ORA) times, and eCrew's IBT contract times in LogTen stay put. Turn it on only for flights that aren't in your eCrew data.
  • Include other airlines' flights, as deadheads is off by default. Turn it on to send United, Delta, SkyWest and other non-Horizon, non-Alaska flights to LogTen as deadheads.
  • Options are the same as on the other pages: Flight number (other airlines get their own code, e.g. UA1164), Aircraft type, Tail and Show.
  • Canceled flights are left out. A diverted flight gets the remark "Diverted"; check its arrival.
  • Flighty's actual arrival is the gate arrival (In), so there's no On-vs-In fix like Flifo's.
  • Matching is the same as everywhere else (date + flight + route), so flights already in LogTen are updated, not copied.

Closing the circle: eCrew → calendar → Flighty → LogTen

Flighty can watch your calendar and add any flight it finds there. Put your trips in that calendar and Flighty tracks every leg for you, then hands the actual times back through its export.

  1. Make the Flights calendar file in Floggy-Go Schedule or Pairing (Flights under Add to Calendar) or in Floggy (Cal – ICS, Flt Cal). It has one event per leg, like ✈ PDX FAT AS 2286, which is the format Flighty reads best.
  2. Add it to its own calendar, for example "QX Flights", in Apple Calendar. Keep Duty and Trip in other calendars so Flighty only sees flights.
  3. Point Flighty at it once: in Flighty, Settings → Calendar Sync, turn on that calendar. Flighty checks it about every 30 minutes and whenever you open the app, and adds each flight it finds.
  4. Fly the trip. Flighty tracks each leg: gates, delays, tail, out and in times.
  5. Close the loop: export from Flighty and run Floggy-Go Flighty. The actual out/in times and tails land on the same LogTen flights your schedule or pairing import created.

Flighty only reads Apple Calendar (and calendars added to it, such as iCloud or Google). For best results keep Background App Refresh on for Flighty and don't force-quit it. Flighty's free tier imports the last 365 days from your calendar; Pro goes back further.

If something's off

  • LogTen didn't open: tap Copy LogTen URL, paste it into Safari's address bar and tap Go.
  • Old version showing: check the version and date in the header. Refresh the page, or on iPad close the home-screen app and reopen it.
  • "Can't read this file": make sure you picked the right report in the right Floggy-Go page. Schedule needs the Personal Crew Schedule Report; Logbook needs the Pilot Logbook.
  • Calendar events in the wrong calendar: pick the calendar in the Add sheet, or set Settings › Apps › Calendar › Default Calendar.
  • Calendar times look off: they're shown in the iPad's current time zone. See Add to Calendar.
  • Duplicate flights in ForeFlight, Logbook Pro or MyFlightbook: those imports add every row. Export each month or trip once.
